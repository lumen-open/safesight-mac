SafeSight Mac watches your screen and covers explicit images and video as soon as they appear: in any app, in any browser, on every display. Everything else is left alone. Your work, your calls, your music and your code carry on as normal.

How it stays on

What makes it different is that it stays on.

Here’s the whole idea in five steps, from the day you set it up to the day you leave.

  1. 1

    Choose your wait

    Install it and pick how long leaving should take, anywhere from a week to a year. You can always make it longer.

  2. 2

    It covers explicit images and video

    It watches your screen and covers them as soon as they appear, on every display. Everything else carries on as normal.

  3. 3

    No off switch for a bad night

    There’s no password to beg for. It’s designed so nobody can unlock it early, not even us.

  4. 4

    Asking to leave starts a countdown

    Change your mind, press Stay, and it’s as if you never asked. Only a friend you choose can shorten the wait, and never to less than a day.

  5. 5

    Then you’re free to go

    When the countdown ends, you can turn it off and remove it. No one to ask and nothing to explain.

Going all in? We’re working on a Hardcore lock, where even wiping your Mac won’t get you out early. The only way around it is Apple’s own proof-of-purchase check, which takes weeks. Its first trial worked: a Mac locked its own recovery with no outside server, and leaving at the end of the wait still worked with that server deleted.

The lock is being built now. See where things stand.

Looking for something for your phone?

We recommend SHIFT. Its Content Filter is free, works on iPhone and Android, and is set up from your computer so it can’t simply be deleted from the phone. We’re not affiliated with SHIFT; we just think it’s good.

Why it exists

Pornography is the most patient thing ever built. It’s free, it never closes, it never runs out, and every inch of it was designed by people who get paid when you stay. It doesn’t have to win the argument. It only has to wait until you’re tired, until it’s late, until the house is quiet and the part of you that knows better has gone to sleep. Then it takes a little more of the person you were trying to become.

Willpower alone rarely beats that, because willpower is the first thing to leave at two in the morning. So SafeSight Mac moves the decision. You make it in daylight, clear-eyed, knowing what you want your life to look like, and it keeps that promise on the nights you can’t. The person you are on your best day gets the final word. The worst hour of your week doesn’t get a vote.

Nobody is watching over your shoulder, nothing is reported to anyone, and there’s no lecture. Wanting this isn’t a confession. It’s the same instinct that makes someone leave their card at home before a night out, or pour the bottle down the sink while they still can. You’re locking a door from the inside. The key is still yours; you’ve just made the walk to it long enough to remember why you locked it.

This is about freedom. Not the thin kind, where you do whatever the next five minutes want, but the real kind: being the same person at midnight that you were at noon.

The usual tricks

Hard to get around, by design

Most blockers fall to a five-minute trick, and everyone who’s needed one knows the tricks. SafeSight Mac is designed so the usual ones don’t work.

The usual ways around a blocker, and what happens with most blockers and with SafeSight Mac

About that last row: wiping is the one way around it we haven’t closed yet. The Hardcore lock is how we’re closing it. If you know macOS well and have ideas for making a wipe harder to use as an escape hatch, we’d love your help. Open an issue or start with CONTRIBUTING.md. If your idea involves a way around the lock, please send it privately through SECURITY.md instead.

What it can’t do

  • Wiping your Mac removes it.
  • It can miss something, and it sometimes covers something harmless.
  • It can’t check protected video, like films in streaming apps, because macOS hides it from screen capture.
  • It only protects this Mac, not your phone, a TV or another computer.
